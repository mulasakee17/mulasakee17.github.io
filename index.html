Moonblade

Engineering prototype

What would it take for the next person—or the next AI—to inherit an understanding of a particular physical object, then check it again when reality changes?

Moonblade explores how a physical object could carry identity, knowledge, task skills, and context for compatible AI. Its current software prototype records what changes, preserves conflicting evidence, and checks when a task can continue.

SwarmAlpha

Research archive · Engineering handoff

What can we learn about collective decisions by changing the information that agents share?

SwarmAlpha explores multi-agent decision processes through information structure and external observation. A historical experiment found an average advantage for attacking over supporting information—and a counterexample helped sharpen the research question.

These projects did not begin as a unified system, and they do not share a technical core. Their questions gradually converged: what must remain visible when intelligence acts, when people disagree, or when understanding passes to someone else?

My approach is to keep the input, the decision, and the evidence close together. A software trace should explain what happened. An experiment should say what was varied and what was measured. A reading path should let another reader return to the source and disagree.

I use AI extensively in research and implementation. I am responsible for the questions, system and experiment boundaries, critical tradeoffs, and acceptance of the work. AI assists with analysis, drafting, implementation, and checks. These pages distinguish an implemented mechanism from an executed test, and both from a result in the physical world.
