When should an anomaly change an explanation?

General reading path · English editorial slice · 7 October 2026

A prediction fails. Should we question the observation, revise the model, or change the research programme?

  1. Karl Popper

    A claim must risk an observable failure. Accepting a falsification also requires a testable refuting effect; a stray contradictory observation alone does not settle the case.

    The Logic of Scientific Discovery, chapter 4, sections 19–22

    Chapter 4, especially section 22; excerpt printed pages 57–66. University-hosted chapter excerpt; underlying excerpt edition not established. Opened without authenticated repository access on 2026-10-07.

  2. Imre Lakatos

    Evaluate the sequence of revisions: does it lead to new predictions that later receive support, or only accommodate facts already known? A young programme may need time; that is not unlimited protection.

    Science and Pseudoscience, 1973 talk

    Four-page transcript, pages 2–4; editorial bracketed additions distinguished. University-hosted transcript of the 1973 Open University talk. Opened without authenticated repository access on 2026-10-07.

  3. Thomas S. Kuhn

    Researchers can share values such as accuracy and scope while applying and weighting them differently. The absence of a compulsory choice algorithm does not make every choice equally defensible.

    Objectivity, Value Judgment, and Theory Choice

    Collection chapter 13; existing collection read checked anthology pages 357–358 and 362–363. Publisher catalogue verifies chapter identity; the catalogue is not the essay text. Publisher catalogue opened on 2026-10-07; this round did not reverify the course scan text.

Where the answers diverge

These are different units and purposes of evaluation: a testable refutation, the progress of a programme, and reasoned choices within a community. The reading path does not fuse them into one verdict procedure.

Role and choices

I choose the questions, source boundaries, and distinctions that make a path worth keeping. AI helps retrieve, compare, draft, and organize material; responsibility for what becomes a durable statement remains mine.

The central choice is to keep an answer connected to its source and conditions. That allows a later reader to inspect the framing and continue reading, rather than inherit a detached summary as authority.

A more distant direction

Jingtu is a design exploration of how these paths might connect real people: AI stays in the background, helping with retrieval, comparison, and encounters. A whitepaper exists; this is not a launched product or evidence of users, financing, or commercial traction.
