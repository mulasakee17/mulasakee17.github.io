The artifact

SwarmAlpha studies collective decision processes: what agents see, how their reports change through discussion, and what an external observer can measure. Controlled interventions make parts of that larger question testable.

The work produced an experimental pipeline, historical comparisons, and an increasingly careful account of what those comparisons can establish. Research is now archived for engineering handoff.

What the experiments changed.

An average effect, a task that went the other way, and a later correction to the explanation. These are reported historical findings, not a new reproduction.

Attacking information improved the average score.

The 15 August 2026 report compared CONTROL, SUPPORTS, and ATTACKS across 45 tasks, one seed, and three arms using deepseek-chat on a hidden benchmark. Lower Brier loss means more accurate probability reports.

Read the historical result and statistical method. This is a benchmark- and model-specific finding from one seed.

The average leaves a counterexample.

On task 17, SUPPORTS scored 0.354 and ATTACKS scored 1.068. Attacking information performed worse on this task.

The useful question becomes when an intervention helps or harms a group—not whether criticism is always beneficial.

Same 15 August report and experiment; task 17 is an individual counterexample, not a second aggregate result.

A narrower explanation did not hold.

The 22 August post-hoc offline bundle analysis corrected the idea that the advantage was specific to rescuing initially wrong groups. In its separate GLM correct-state analysis, GAP_AS = SUPPORTS − ATTACKS was +0.3349, with reported 95% CI [0.0883, 0.6868].

The ATTACKS–SUPPORTS difference also appeared in the GLM correct-state stratum (12 task/seed blocks). Without a frozen interaction test, the evidence did not support a claim that the effect existed only in wrong states.

Read the follow-up and revised interpretation. Different analysis and model; do not pool it with the 45-task deepseek-chat comparison. GAP_AS has the opposite sign convention to the chart above.

The three-arm comparison contract

Public design and manifest · Inspected 7 October 2026

Paired block: task × seed. Statistical unit: task. The conditions below continue from a shared discussion state.

What makes a block comparable?

The frozen analysis requires one row per condition, matching task, seed, model, resolution and Round-1 state hash; actual Round-1 probability arrays must match. Final Brier is recomputed from final agent beliefs.

A contrast needs non-null final Brier in all three conditions. Incomplete blocks remain missing; they are not set to zero, repaired or imputed.

The three-arm comparison design below is a distinct documented analysis surface. Model self-labels describe the implemented information bundle; probability reports are observable outputs, rather than direct measurements of internal cognition. This website transcribes public reports and did not run new experiments or recompute their results.

Separate the intervention from the explanation

Changing the information agents see can reveal a response without settling why it happened. The follow-up’s correction matters because an attractive “targeted rescue” story was narrower than the evidence allowed. Keeping the condition, measured output, and interpretation separate makes that correction visible.

I own the research question, experiment boundaries, and interpretation, with AI assistance in analysis, implementation, drafting, and checks. The historical reports preserve the decisions and their qualifications. Read the research identity and design philosophy.

What would be valuable next?

The next useful conversation is about the mechanism: which information, for which initial group state, under which matched conditions? The archive keeps the units, missing-data treatment, and interpretation available for that inspection.
