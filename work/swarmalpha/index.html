How does information exposure change probability reports and offline Brier loss in an LLM multi-agent experiment?

SwarmAlpha · Research archive / Engineering handoff

The artifact

SwarmAlpha provides a controlled experimental pipeline and documented research surface. The intervention changes the information available to agents; the measured outputs are probability reports and an offline scoring loss.

The distinction matters: these measurements do not reveal internal cognition, isolate pure social influence, or validate a general governance strategy. Research has stopped; the repository now records its boundary and engineering handoff.

Read the experiment at its boundary.

A design is useful evidence when the treatment, unit of analysis, and measured output are explicit. No result curve is drawn here without a releasable, recomputable dataset.

The three-arm comparison contract

Public design and manifest · Inspected 7 October 2026

Unit: A task × seed block. The conditions below continue from a shared discussion state.

Condition What is shown
CONTROL The baseline continuation used in the existing fork comparison.
SUPPORTS The implemented disclosure bundle of model-self-labeled supporting relations.
ATTACKS The implemented disclosure bundle of model-self-labeled attacking relations.

The frozen analysis requires one row per condition, matching task, seed, model, resolution and Round-1 state hash; actual Round-1 probability arrays must match. Final Brier is recomputed from final agent beliefs.

A contrast needs non-null final Brier in all three conditions. Incomplete blocks remain missing; they are not set to zero, repaired or imputed.

This is a descriptive post-hoc complete-block comparison. Model self-labels are not oracle relations, and a probability change is not a measurement of internal cognition or pure social influence. This website task inspected documentation and a manifest; it did not run new experiments or reproduce numerical results.

Role and choices

I own the research question, experiment boundaries, and interpretation of the outputs, with AI assistance in analysis, implementation, drafting, and checks. The key choice is to separate the exposure condition from the score, and to preserve the record needed to check how a comparison was formed.

A probability report is an observable output. The Brier loss scores that output against the task outcome; it should not be renamed a measurement of an agent’s internal beliefs. Missing, failed, primary, and recovery runs require separate accounting.

What would be valuable next?

Independent inspection of the experiment’s units, matched conditions, missing-data treatment, and interpretation would be more useful than a broader claim. The archive is a basis for that conversation, not a claim of publication or acceptance.
