How do partial views
become a collective judgment?

SwarmAlpha is my experimental research into how AI agents with different local information form collective judgments—and what an outside observer can learn from the process.

Research Question

A group of agents can see different evidence, disagree, and revise its reports through discussion. How does that local information become a collective judgment? Which changes in the group can we observe and describe from outside its conversation?

I approached these questions by building an observation and experiment layer around the agents, then testing what happens when the information available to them changes.

What I Built

The work produced a multi-agent experiment system with controlled information disclosure, comparisons branching from a shared discussion state, and records of actual model runs. It also produced tools for describing group processes through information exposure, evidence use, source records, responses, and resource use.

The archive brings together reusable code, experiment designs, and measurement contracts. I set the research direction, experiment boundaries, and interpretation, with AI assisting in implementation and analysis. The process descriptor is implemented and structurally checked; its empirical validity remains a research question.

Research & Findings

Experiments with real models found that information disclosure can change agents’ reported judgments. In a post-hoc replication across 45 tasks and two seeds with deepseek-chat, disclosing attacking relations produced lower average Brier loss than disclosing supporting relations, with individual tasks showing the reverse. This measured probability reports; it did not establish a general rule for choosing interventions. Read the experiment report.

Some early predictive hypotheses were not supported by later checks. The research consequently moved toward more careful descriptions of group states and clearer measurement contracts. The README and reports preserve that evolution, including negative findings and the limits of the experiments. Active research has stopped; the code and records remain available for further study and engineering handoff.
