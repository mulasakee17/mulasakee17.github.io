Moonblade

Engineering prototype

Watching classmates assemble a drone at summer camp left me wondering: how could AI reliably know the particular object in front of it?

Moonblade explores an AI interface for physical objects: their identity, knowledge, task skills, and changing context. The current software prototype preserves conflicting observations and checks when a task can continue.

SwarmAlpha

Multi-agent decision research

SwarmAlpha began with financial prediction and multi-agent collaboration. It became a study of how information changes agents’ reported judgments.

The research archive contains the experiment system, actual model runs, and findings that changed my questions. Active research has stopped; the code and records remain available.

Read the case

Information disclosure can change a group’s reported judgment.

Experiments compared what agents reported after receiving different information. The results prompted further work on how to describe and measure the group’s changing state.

The Ancestors

Personal reading collection · Public reading path

When I’m unsure what to think about a question in my own life, I want to know how others have faced it before.

In The Ancestors, I read earlier thinkers’ works and disagreements, and keep track of how my own questions and judgments change. This page opens one path through that collection.

Read the case

Am I building the life I want—or the life others have taught me to admire?

A life can look successful from the outside and still leave its owner unsure. Read with Adam Smith, Harry Frankfurt, and John Stuart Mill: what deserves admiration, which desires to stand behind, and what choosing for oneself requires.

Follow the question

Learning as I go.

These projects had different beginnings. Working on them has taught me to ask more precise questions: what does the software actually do, what did an experiment measure, and where does an interpretation come from?

I’m still building the technical foundations I need to understand an approach and judge whether it fits. I keep traces, experiment reports, and original sources here so that other people can check my work and reach a different conclusion.

I use AI extensively for research, drafting, implementation, and checks. I choose the questions, define system and experiment boundaries, make the key tradeoffs, and decide whether the results meet the criteria. Learning to judge the assistance is part of the work.

About

Alongside high school, I’m making time for technical projects, experiments, and long-term reading. I want to finish things I can actually examine, while learning enough to understand how they work.

Often I start with a question that sounds simple. Trying to build or test something shows me which parts I have not understood, and sometimes changes the question itself. This site keeps a record of that process.

Earlier work includes Personal Agent Runtime, which separates a model’s proposed action from permission and verification, and the Phase Reversal Hypothesis. RIZHICHI remains accessible as a secondary page.
